# Contributor License Agreement

> **Not reviewed by a lawyer.** This is written from the conventional shape of
> a contributor agreement so there is something concrete to correct, and it is
> good enough to keep the relicensing option open while the project is small.
> Have it reviewed before relying on it for a commercial licence sale.

By contributing to RedaSMS you agree to the following. It applies to code,
documentation, translations, configuration, and anything else you submit.

## 1. You keep your copyright

You are not signing your work away. You keep every right you have in your
contribution and may use it however you like, including in other projects.

## 2. What you grant

You grant Reda Ekengren, and anyone who receives the software from him, a
perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable, royalty-free licence to use, reproduce,
modify, publish, sublicense and distribute your contribution, **including the
right to distribute it under licence terms other than the AGPL-3.0**.

That last clause is the point of this document. RedaSMS is AGPL-3.0, and
the AGPL obliges anyone running a modified version as a network service to
offer its source to that service's users. Some organisations cannot accept
that, so a commercial licence has to be available. Selling one requires holding
the rights to every line, and once a contribution arrives under AGPL alone
those rights are split. Then a single contributor who cannot be reached, or who
declines, blocks it for everyone.

You also grant a patent licence covering any patent claim of yours that your
contribution necessarily infringes.

## 3. What you are confirming

- The contribution is your own work, or you have the right to submit it.
- It is not covered by an agreement with an employer or client that would
  conflict with this. If your employer has rights to your work, get their
  permission first.
- You are not knowingly including anyone else's code without saying so and
  naming its licence.

## 4. No warranty

You provide your contribution as is, with no warranty of any kind.

## How to sign

Open a pull request. A bot will comment with a one-line reply to post, and it
records that you signed. You do this once, not per pull request.

Automated dependency updates are exempt: they are changes to lock files made by
this repository's own bot, not contributions from a person.

## Why this exists at all

Every revenue path this project might take, other than "give it away and hope",
depends on being able to license it more than one way: a hosted service, a
support contract, a consultancy operating it for many customers, an
organisation that needs an exception from the AGPL. A CLA in place before the
first outside contribution costs nothing. Afterwards it cannot be added
retroactively.
